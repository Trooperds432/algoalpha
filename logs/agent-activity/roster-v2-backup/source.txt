3.1 Chief Executive Officer — CEO

Reports to: Board of Directors.

Role purpose: Provide overall strategic, commercial and organisational leadership, ensuring that the firm operates as a financially sustainable, appropriately governed global investment management business.

Principal responsibilities

Develop and implement the firm's long-term corporate strategy, investment business model and geographical expansion plans.
Establish firm-wide objectives covering investment performance, assets under management, profitability, operational resilience and institutional development.
Allocate financial, technological and human resources among investment strategies and business divisions.
Chair the executive management committee and coordinate the activities of all C-suite executives.
Approve major strategic initiatives, acquisitions, partnerships and material capital expenditures within delegated authority.
Oversee succession planning, senior management performance and organisational culture.
Maintain relationships with shareholders, institutional investors, regulators and strategic counterparties.
Ensure that business growth does not compromise regulatory compliance, financial stability or fiduciary responsibilities.

Required deliverables: Corporate strategic plan, annual business objectives, executive performance reviews, capital expenditure proposals and board reports.

Performance indicators: Sustainable profitability, achievement of strategic objectives, organisational resilience, client retention and effectiveness of corporate governance.

3.2 Chief Investment Officer — CIO

Reports to: CEO and Board Investment Committee.

Role purpose: Direct the firm's investment activities and establish a coherent investment framework governing portfolio management, strategy selection and capital deployment.

Principal responsibilities

Define the firm's investment philosophy, return objectives and portfolio management framework.
Supervise portfolio managers, investment researchers and capital allocation functions.
Establish investment mandates and recommend risk budgets consistent with the firm's approved risk appetite.
Review proposed investment strategies and assess their economic rationale, expected returns and portfolio implications.
Chair the Investment Committee and oversee the investment approval process.
Coordinate capital allocation across equities, futures, options, fixed income, commodities and alternative investments.
Monitor aggregate investment performance and investigate material deviations from investment objectives.
Ensure that investment decisions are supported by independently validated research.

Required deliverables: Investment policy statements, approved investment mandates, strategy allocation recommendations and consolidated investment performance reports.

Performance indicators: Mandate-adjusted investment performance, portfolio diversification, capital efficiency and adherence to approved investment limits.

3.3 Chief Operating Officer — COO

Reports to: CEO.

Role purpose: Direct the firm's operational infrastructure and ensure the reliable execution of investment, trading, settlement and administrative processes.

Principal responsibilities

Supervise trade operations, settlements, reconciliations and fund administration.
Establish standard operating procedures and service-level agreements across business divisions.
Coordinate operational dependencies between investment teams, technology, finance and compliance.
Manage outsourced service providers and operational counterparties.
Develop business continuity and disaster recovery arrangements.
Oversee operational incident management and remediation.
Identify process inefficiencies and implement appropriate workflow automation.
Maintain operational controls that support accurate transaction processing and financial reporting.

Required deliverables: Operating procedures, operational risk reports, business continuity plans and service performance dashboards.

Performance indicators: Settlement accuracy, operational availability, processing efficiency, incident resolution and operational cost efficiency.

3.4 Chief Technology Officer — CTO

Reports to: CEO.

Role purpose: Establish the firm's technology strategy and maintain the infrastructure supporting research, trading, data processing and investment operations.

Principal responsibilities

Develop the enterprise technology roadmap and systems architecture.
Supervise software engineering, quantitative development, infrastructure and platform operations.
Oversee the construction of research environments, backtesting engines, execution systems and portfolio management platforms.
Establish technology standards covering reliability, scalability, interoperability and performance.
Coordinate infrastructure requirements with investment and quantitative research teams.
Oversee technology procurement, third-party platforms and systems integration.
Implement technology change-management and disaster recovery procedures.
Ensure that production systems can support the firm's required trading frequencies and operational workloads.

Required deliverables: Technology roadmap, architecture standards, infrastructure investment proposals and systems performance reports.

Performance indicators: System availability, computational efficiency, deployment reliability, technical debt and platform scalability.

3.5 Chief Risk Officer — CRO

Reports to: CEO, with direct access to the Board Risk Committee.

Role purpose: Provide independent oversight of investment, market, liquidity, counterparty and operational risks.

Principal responsibilities

Establish the enterprise risk management framework.
Recommend portfolio and firm-wide risk limits.
Oversee market risk, liquidity risk, leverage, concentration and counterparty exposures.
Develop stress-testing and scenario-analysis programmes.
Independently review proposed investment strategies and material portfolio changes.
Supervise risk monitoring across discretionary and systematic investment activities.
Establish procedures for risk breaches, trading suspensions and emergency exposure reductions.
Report material risk matters directly to the Risk Committee.

Required deliverables: Risk appetite proposals, risk-limit schedules, stress-testing reports and independent portfolio risk assessments.

Performance indicators: Risk-limit compliance, risk identification effectiveness, escalation timeliness and quality of risk reporting.

Authority: May suspend trading or require exposure reductions within powers formally delegated by the firm's governing body.

3.6 Chief Compliance Officer — CCO

Reports to: CEO and Board Compliance Committee.

Role purpose: Establish and supervise the firm's regulatory compliance framework across all operating jurisdictions.

Principal responsibilities

Maintain the firm's compliance policies and regulatory obligations register.
Oversee market-conduct controls, conflicts of interest and personal account dealing policies.
Supervise regulatory reporting, financial crime compliance and trade surveillance.
Review proposed investment products, trading activities and external commercial arrangements.
Coordinate regulatory examinations, investigations and compliance reviews.
Establish procedures governing the lawful use of market data and alternative data.
Monitor regulatory developments affecting investment management and automated trading.
Oversee compliance training and the investigation of potential regulatory breaches.

Required deliverables: Compliance monitoring programme, regulatory submissions, breach reports and annual compliance assessments.

Performance indicators: Timely regulatory reporting, effectiveness of monitoring, remediation completion and compliance with applicable obligations.

3.7 Chief Financial Officer — CFO

Reports to: CEO.

Role purpose: Direct the firm's corporate financial management, treasury, accounting and financial planning activities.

Principal responsibilities

Manage corporate budgeting, financial forecasting and capital planning.
Oversee financial accounting, statutory reporting and tax coordination.
Supervise treasury operations and corporate liquidity management.
Establish financial controls governing expenditure, revenue recognition and financial reporting.
Evaluate the financial implications of investment in technology, personnel and infrastructure.
Monitor corporate profitability, operating margins and financing requirements.
Coordinate external audits and financial relationships with banks and service providers.
Oversee appropriate separation between corporate finances and client or fund assets.

Required deliverables: Annual budgets, financial statements, treasury forecasts and management accounts.

Performance indicators: Financial reporting accuracy, budget adherence, corporate liquidity and cost efficiency.

3.8 Chief Data Officer — CDO

Reports to: CEO, working closely with the CTO and CIO.

Role purpose: Establish the firm's enterprise data strategy and ensure that investment research, risk management and trading systems operate on reliable, appropriately governed information.

Principal responsibilities

Develop the firm's market data and alternative data acquisition strategy.
Establish enterprise data governance and ownership standards.
Supervise data engineering, reference data, data quality and historical data management.
Approve data classification, retention and access policies within delegated authority.
Oversee data licensing, vendor evaluation and information provenance.
Establish quality standards for historical and real-time datasets.
Ensure that research datasets support point-in-time analysis and reproducibility.
Coordinate data requirements across investment, research, risk and technology divisions.

Required deliverables: Enterprise data strategy, data governance policies, vendor assessments and data quality reports.

Performance indicators: Data accuracy, availability, licensing compliance, lineage completeness and data acquisition efficiency.

4. Portfolio Management Division

Division mandate: Manage investment capital across approved asset classes and strategies, construct portfolios consistent with client mandates and generate risk-adjusted investment returns.

Division head: Chief Investment Officer.

Portfolio managers possess investment discretion only within approved mandates and risk budgets. Independent risk functions retain authority over the monitoring and enforcement of applicable controls.

Professional requirements

Portfolio management positions generally require advanced knowledge of financial markets, portfolio theory, risk management, investment instruments and portfolio performance measurement.

Relevant professional qualifications may include the CFA designation, appropriate regulatory qualifications and advanced degrees in finance, economics, mathematics or quantitative disciplines.

4.1 Global Portfolio Manager

Mandate: Manage diversified portfolios across international markets, asset classes and currencies.

Responsibilities: Establish global asset allocation and geographical exposures; integrate macroeconomic, fundamental and quantitative research; manage currency and country risk; coordinate specialist portfolio managers; and rebalance portfolios as investment conditions evolve.

Deliverables: Global allocation plans, consolidated portfolio reviews and geographical exposure reports.

KPIs: Mandate-adjusted return, tracking error, diversification effectiveness and portfolio risk compliance.

4.2 Equities Portfolio Manager

Mandate: Manage discretionary or systematic equity portfolios.

Responsibilities: Evaluate company fundamentals, valuations and expected earnings; integrate quantitative signals; construct equity positions; manage sector and factor exposures; oversee corporate-action implications; and coordinate equity execution.

Deliverables: Equity investment proposals, position-sizing decisions and portfolio attribution reports.

KPIs: Benchmark-relative performance, information ratio, stock-selection contribution and transaction-cost efficiency.

4.3 Futures Portfolio Manager

Mandate: Manage portfolios of exchange-traded futures across financial and commodity markets.

Responsibilities: Determine directional and relative-value futures exposures; monitor contract specifications, expiries and margin requirements; manage rolling schedules; evaluate futures curves and basis; and coordinate collateral management.

Deliverables: Futures exposure plans, contract roll schedules and margin utilisation reports.

KPIs: Risk-adjusted return, realised execution costs, collateral efficiency and compliance with margin and leverage limits.

4.4 Options Portfolio Manager

Mandate: Manage portfolios of listed or approved OTC options.

Responsibilities: Construct options strategies based on expected volatility, market direction and risk-reward characteristics; oversee pricing-model assumptions; monitor Greeks; manage expiry and exercise risk; and coordinate dynamic hedging.

Deliverables: Options strategy proposals, Greeks exposure reports and hedge adjustment plans.

KPIs: Risk-adjusted P&L, hedge effectiveness, volatility exposure management and tail-loss containment.

4.5 ETF Portfolio Manager

Mandate: Manage portfolios containing exchange-traded funds and related instruments.

Responsibilities: Select ETFs based on benchmark exposure, fund structure, liquidity, fees and tracking characteristics; monitor premiums and discounts to NAV; manage portfolio rebalancing; and coordinate efficient ETF execution.

Deliverables: ETF selection reports, portfolio tracking assessments and liquidity analyses.

KPIs: Tracking error, total ownership cost, portfolio diversification and execution quality.

4.6 Fixed Income Portfolio Manager

Mandate: Manage portfolios of government, corporate and other approved fixed income securities.

Responsibilities: Establish interest-rate and credit exposures; manage duration, convexity and yield-curve positioning; evaluate issuer creditworthiness; oversee liquidity and maturity profiles; and implement approved interest-rate hedges.

Deliverables: Fixed income allocation plans, duration reports and credit exposure reviews.

KPIs: Risk-adjusted return, duration compliance, credit-loss experience and benchmark-relative performance.

4.7 Macro Portfolio Manager

Mandate: Manage cross-asset strategies informed by global macroeconomic developments.

Responsibilities: Analyse monetary policy, inflation, growth, fiscal policy and capital flows; translate macroeconomic views into approved equity-index, rates, FX and commodity exposures; and construct scenario-dependent portfolio positions.

Deliverables: Global macro investment thesis, cross-asset positioning reports and scenario analyses.

KPIs: Risk-adjusted portfolio return, scenario performance, exposure discipline and macro attribution.

4.8 Volatility Portfolio Manager

Mandate: Manage strategies whose performance depends on realised volatility, implied volatility or volatility risk premia.

Responsibilities: Evaluate volatility surfaces, term structures and skew; manage volatility-linked instruments; monitor convexity and Greeks exposures; coordinate hedging; and assess portfolio sensitivity to discontinuous market movements.

Deliverables: Volatility strategy plans, Greeks reports and volatility scenario analyses.

KPIs: Risk-adjusted P&L, hedge costs, exposure accuracy and performance across volatility regimes.

4.9 Commodities Portfolio Manager

Mandate: Manage exposure to approved energy, metals and agricultural commodity markets.

Responsibilities: Analyse physical supply and demand, inventories, seasonal patterns, transportation constraints and futures curves; evaluate commodity-specific risks; manage contract rolls and collateral; and coordinate execution.

Deliverables: Commodity market outlooks, futures allocation plans and supply-demand research reports.

KPIs: Risk-adjusted return, carry and roll attribution, drawdown control and transaction-cost efficiency.

4.10 Crypto Portfolio Manager

Mandate: Manage permitted digital-asset investments and related instruments within the firm's applicable regulatory framework.

Responsibilities: Evaluate digital-asset market structure, custody arrangements, exchange and counterparty exposure, liquidity, tokenomics and blockchain-specific risks; manage portfolio concentrations; and coordinate secure execution and asset custody.

Deliverables: Digital-asset investment proposals, custody risk assessments and portfolio exposure reports.

KPIs: Risk-adjusted return, liquidity management, custody integrity and compliance with approved investment restrictions.

Additional requirement: No digital-asset activity may commence without confirmation of the applicable legal permissions, custody arrangements and operational safeguards.

4.11 Capital Allocation Manager

Mandate: Allocate available investment capital across approved portfolio managers and strategies.

Responsibilities: Evaluate expected returns, drawdowns, capacity, correlations and marginal portfolio risk; recommend initial capital allocations and subsequent reallocations; and monitor capital utilisation.

Deliverables: Capital allocation recommendations, strategy-level capital budgets and reallocation analyses.

KPIs: Capital efficiency, portfolio diversification, risk-budget utilisation and allocation-adjusted performance.

4.12 Portfolio Construction Manager

Mandate: Convert approved investment forecasts and exposures into investable portfolios.

Responsibilities: Establish portfolio weighting methodologies; integrate expected returns, covariance estimates, factor exposures and liquidity constraints; coordinate portfolio rebalancing; and evaluate concentration and diversification.

Deliverables: Target portfolio weights, construction methodology documents and portfolio constraint reports.

KPIs: Risk-adjusted performance, constraint compliance, concentration management and turnover efficiency.

4.13 Strategy Allocation Manager

Mandate: Determine the allocation of capital among approved trading and investment strategies.

Responsibilities: Evaluate strategy performance, correlation, regime sensitivity, expected capacity and execution costs; recommend strategy combinations; and identify excessive dependence on common sources of return.

Deliverables: Strategy allocation proposals, correlation analyses and strategy performance reviews.

KPIs: Diversification benefit, capital utilisation, realised strategy contribution and allocation stability.

4.14 Portfolio Optimisation Manager

Mandate: Develop and maintain mathematical methods for portfolio weight optimisation.

Responsibilities: Implement mean-variance, risk-parity, Black–Litterman and other approved optimisation methodologies; incorporate transaction costs, leverage, liquidity and exposure constraints; evaluate estimation uncertainty; and test sensitivity to modelling assumptions.

Deliverables: Optimisation models, recommended portfolio weights, constraint diagnostics and sensitivity analyses.

KPIs: Optimisation reliability, expected-to-realised risk alignment, turnover efficiency and portfolio constraint compliance.

5. Quantitative Research Division

Division mandate: Develop empirically supported investment hypotheses, statistical models and systematic trading strategies.

Division head: Quantitative Research Director.

Core research principle: A statistical relationship does not constitute an investable trading strategy until its economic rationale, robustness, implementation feasibility and expected net performance have been evaluated.

Research should include controls against look-ahead bias, survivorship bias, inappropriate multiple testing and unrealistic assumptions regarding execution.

The division operates independently from the Strategy Validation Division, which performs formal model challenge and approval assessments.

5.1 Quantitative Research Director

Mandate: Direct the firm's quantitative research programme and supervise the development of systematic investment strategies.

Responsibilities: Establish research priorities, allocate research resources, approve research methodologies, oversee reproducibility standards and coordinate the progression of research candidates into independent validation.

Deliverables: Research roadmap, strategy development pipeline and research quality reports.

KPIs: Validated strategy output, research reproducibility, research efficiency and implementation success.

5.2 Alpha Researcher

Mandate: Identify and develop investment signals capable of generating incremental expected returns.

Responsibilities: Formulate economic or statistical hypotheses; investigate predictive features; construct trading signals; estimate transaction-cost-adjusted returns; and test whether signals contribute incremental information beyond existing strategies.

Deliverables: Alpha research memorandum, signal specification, reproducible code and preliminary backtest.

KPIs: Out-of-sample signal quality, incremental portfolio contribution, turnover-adjusted returns and signal stability.

5.3 Statistical Arbitrage Researcher

Mandate: Develop market-neutral or controlled-exposure strategies based on relative pricing relationships.

Responsibilities: Investigate cointegration, residual returns, relative valuation and cross-sectional anomalies; construct long-short trading signals; model convergence behaviour; and evaluate financing, short availability and execution costs.

Deliverables: Statistical arbitrage models, spread diagnostics and market-neutral backtests.

KPIs: Net risk-adjusted return, residual factor exposure, convergence stability and implementation capacity.

5.4 Mean Reversion Researcher

Mandate: Identify statistically supported opportunities arising from temporary price deviations.

Responsibilities: Develop stationarity and equilibrium diagnostics; estimate reversion speeds and half-lives; construct entry and exit rules; and test strategy behaviour during structural breaks and persistent trends.

Deliverables: Mean-reversion signal models, equilibrium diagnostics and regime-conditioned backtests.

KPIs: Out-of-sample performance, reversion stability, false-entry frequency and drawdown control.

5.5 Momentum Researcher

Mandate: Develop strategies based on persistent trends in prices, returns or other market variables.

Responsibilities: Research time-series and cross-sectional momentum; identify appropriate signal horizons; evaluate trend persistence; develop volatility-adjusted position-sizing methods; and assess trend-reversal risk.

Deliverables: Momentum models, trend classification methods and strategy performance reports.

KPIs: Out-of-sample return, trend capture, turnover-adjusted profitability and reversal-loss management.

5.6 Volatility Researcher

Mandate: Research volatility dynamics and develop predictive models of future market variability.

Responsibilities: Estimate realised and conditional volatility; investigate volatility clustering, asymmetry and volatility risk premia; develop GARCH-family and other forecasting models; and compare forecasts against appropriate benchmarks.

Deliverables: Volatility forecasts, model comparison reports and volatility-regime analyses.

KPIs: Forecast accuracy, predictive stability, calibration and downstream portfolio contribution.

5.7 Options Researcher

Mandate: Develop quantitative models and trading strategies involving options.

Responsibilities: Research implied volatility surfaces, option pricing, skew, term structure, Greeks and relative-value opportunities; evaluate model assumptions and hedging costs; and design options strategies within approved risk constraints.

Deliverables: Options pricing models, volatility surface analyses and strategy research reports.

KPIs: Pricing accuracy, hedging-model quality, out-of-sample strategy performance and robustness to volatility shocks.

5.8 ETF Researcher

Mandate: Investigate ETF market behaviour and develop ETF-related investment signals.

Responsibilities: Analyse ETF premiums and discounts, benchmark tracking, flows, liquidity and constituent relationships; research relative-value strategies; and evaluate creation-redemption mechanics and execution feasibility.

Deliverables: ETF signal models, fair-value estimates and ETF market structure research.

KPIs: Signal stability, net strategy performance, tracking model accuracy and implementation costs.

5.9 Regime Detection Researcher

Mandate: Develop statistical models identifying changes in market conditions and relationships among financial assets.

Responsibilities: Construct Hidden Markov Models, Gaussian mixture models and other regime-classification approaches; estimate state probabilities and transition dynamics; evaluate model stability; and investigate how strategy performance varies across market conditions.

Deliverables: Regime classification models, transition probability matrices, regime-conditioned risk reports and strategy allocation research.

KPIs: Out-of-sample regime classification quality, model stability, calibration and incremental value to investment decisions.

Important requirement: Regime labels must be validated against economically meaningful characteristics rather than assuming that an unsupervised state automatically represents a bullish, bearish or sideways market.

Two Sigma's published research provides an example of using Gaussian mixture models to identify market regimes from multiple factor-return series. This is a methodological reference rather than evidence that any specific regime model will improve trading returns.

5.10 Factor Researcher

Mandate: Research systematic investment factors and evaluate their explanatory and predictive properties.

Responsibilities: Develop value, momentum, quality, carry, volatility and other factor definitions; estimate factor returns and exposures; investigate interactions and crowding; and distinguish factor premia from implementation-dependent anomalies.

Deliverables: Factor models, factor performance studies and exposure attribution reports.

KPIs: Factor robustness, incremental explanatory power, investability and out-of-sample stability.

5.11 Machine Learning Researcher

Mandate: Develop machine learning models for financial prediction, classification and signal generation.

Responsibilities: Implement supervised and unsupervised learning methodologies; design appropriate time-series validation procedures; optimise model hyperparameters without contaminating test samples; evaluate calibration and explainability; and compare complex models against simpler statistical benchmarks.

Deliverables: Trained models, model cards, reproducible experiments and predictive performance reports.

KPIs: Out-of-sample predictive performance, model stability, computational efficiency and incremental investment value.

5.12 Market Microstructure Researcher

Mandate: Research the mechanisms governing price formation, liquidity and transaction costs.

Responsibilities: Analyse order-book dynamics, bid-ask spreads, order flow, liquidity imbalances and price impact; develop short-horizon predictive features; and evaluate execution strategy performance under realistic trading conditions.

Deliverables: Microstructure models, liquidity forecasts and execution research reports.

KPIs: Predictive accuracy, execution-cost reductions, simulation fidelity and model stability.

5.13 Alternative Data Researcher

Mandate: Identify and evaluate non-traditional datasets that may contain investment-relevant information.

Responsibilities: Research satellite, transaction, shipping, supply-chain, website, sentiment and other approved data sources; establish lawful data acquisition methods; evaluate signal relevance; and identify publication lags, sampling biases and changes in coverage.

Deliverables: Alternative data investment hypotheses, dataset assessments and incremental signal studies.

KPIs: Validated signal contribution, data reliability, cost efficiency and compliance with data usage restrictions.

5.14 Time Series Researcher

Mandate: Develop statistical models for financial observations recorded through time.

Responsibilities: Analyse stationarity, autocorrelation, heteroskedasticity and structural breaks; construct forecasting models; evaluate temporal dependence; and develop appropriate backtesting procedures for financial time series.

Deliverables: Forecasting models, diagnostic reports and time-series research libraries.

KPIs: Forecast reliability, model stability, reproducibility and out-of-sample performance.

5.15 Signal Combination Researcher

Mandate: Construct composite investment signals from multiple approved predictive models.

Responsibilities: Measure signal correlation, information overlap and conditional effectiveness; develop ensemble weighting methods; investigate signal disagreement; and control excessive exposure to common underlying factors.

Deliverables: Composite signal specifications, ensemble models and signal contribution analyses.

KPIs: Incremental predictive improvement, turnover-adjusted returns and signal diversification.

5.16 Feature Engineering Researcher

Mandate: Develop informative, reproducible and temporally valid features for quantitative investment models.

Responsibilities: Construct financial, technical, fundamental and alternative-data features; manage transformations and normalisation; evaluate feature stability; prevent future-information leakage; and coordinate integration into the approved feature store.

Deliverables: Feature definitions, transformation code, feature lineage records and feature quality reports.

KPIs: Feature reproducibility, predictive contribution, availability and temporal integrity.

5.17 Research Replication Agent

Mandate: Independently reproduce published and internally developed quantitative research.

Responsibilities: Reconstruct model specifications from research documents; retrieve permitted datasets; reproduce calculations and experiments; compare reported results with independent implementations; and document inconsistencies or missing methodological details.

Deliverables: Replication packages, reproducibility reports and discrepancy logs.

KPIs: Replication accuracy, completeness of experimental documentation and reproducibility of results.

Authority: May recommend rejection or further investigation of research that cannot be independently reproduced.

5.18 Research Peer Reviewer

Mandate: Evaluate the scientific and economic quality of quantitative research before formal strategy validation.

Responsibilities: Review hypotheses, data selection, statistical methods, model assumptions, experimental design and interpretation; challenge unsupported conclusions; identify possible biases; and recommend additional research.

Deliverables: Peer-review reports, methodological challenge records and research revision recommendations.

KPIs: Review quality, identification of material research weaknesses and completeness of methodological assessment.

5.19 Research Librarian

Mandate: Maintain the firm's institutional knowledge base and quantitative research repository.

Responsibilities: Catalogue academic publications, research memoranda, models, datasets and experiments; maintain metadata and version histories; manage research citations; and ensure approved research resources are discoverable and reproducible.

Deliverables: Searchable research repository, literature reviews and research lineage records.

KPIs: Repository completeness, retrieval accuracy, knowledge reuse and documentation quality.

6. Fundamental Research Division

Division mandate: Produce evidence-based assessments of companies, sectors, industries, macroeconomic conditions and investment catalysts.

Division head: Head of Fundamental Research, reporting to the CIO.

Research analysts must distinguish reported facts, management guidance, market expectations and independent forecasts.

Every investment recommendation must contain a documented investment thesis, valuation framework, principal risks and conditions under which the thesis would no longer be valid.

6.1 Equity Analyst

Mandate: Conduct fundamental research on publicly traded companies.

Responsibilities: Analyse financial statements, competitive positioning, management guidance, cash flows and valuation; develop earnings forecasts; construct DCF and relative valuation models; and identify investment catalysts and downside risks.

Deliverables: Company research reports, financial models, investment recommendations and valuation sensitivity analyses.

KPIs: Forecast accuracy, research timeliness, investment contribution and analytical quality.

6.2 Sector Analyst

Mandate: Evaluate investment opportunities and structural developments within an assigned market sector.

Responsibilities: Analyse sector profitability, valuation, competitive dynamics, regulation, demand trends and business cycles; compare companies; and identify sector-level risks and opportunities.

Deliverables: Sector outlook reports, relative valuation analyses and sector allocation recommendations.

KPIs: Research accuracy, identification of material sector developments and contribution to investment decisions.

6.3 Macro Research Analyst

Mandate: Analyse economic conditions and their implications for financial markets.

Responsibilities: Research inflation, employment, growth, interest rates, monetary policy, fiscal policy and global capital flows; construct economic scenarios; and evaluate cross-asset transmission mechanisms.

Deliverables: Macroeconomic forecasts, policy analysis reports and scenario-based market research.

KPIs: Forecast calibration, research relevance, timeliness and consistency of economic analysis.

6.4 Earnings Analyst

Mandate: Analyse corporate earnings announcements and their implications for security valuations.

Responsibilities: Review earnings releases, financial statements, guidance and earnings-call transcripts; compare reported figures against consensus expectations; identify changes in margins, demand and capital expenditure; and update forward financial estimates.

Deliverables: Earnings preview reports, post-results analyses and earnings revision models.

KPIs: Forecast accuracy, reporting speed, identification of material estimate changes and investment relevance.

6.5 Industry Analyst

Mandate: Evaluate the economic structure and competitive conditions of specific industries.

Responsibilities: Analyse supply chains, industry capacity, market share, technological changes, cost structures and regulatory developments; identify structural growth constraints and competitive advantages; and assess industry-level investment implications.

Deliverables: Industry research reports, competitive landscape assessments and supply-chain analyses.

KPIs: Research coverage, analytical accuracy and identification of material industry developments.

6.6 News Intelligence Analyst

Mandate: Identify and interpret public news developments relevant to the firm's investment portfolios.

Responsibilities: Monitor approved financial news sources, corporate announcements and regulatory publications; classify events; verify source reliability; assess potential investment implications; and distribute time-sensitive alerts.

Deliverables: Market intelligence briefs, event alerts and source-linked news summaries.

KPIs: Alert timeliness, factual accuracy, event relevance and false-positive frequency.

6.7 Corporate Actions Researcher

Mandate: Analyse corporate events that may materially affect investment securities.

Responsibilities: Investigate mergers, acquisitions, dividends, stock splits, rights issues, spin-offs and restructurings; estimate economic and valuation implications; and coordinate with portfolio management and operational corporate-actions teams.

Deliverables: Corporate-action research reports, event impact analyses and portfolio adjustment recommendations.

KPIs: Event coverage accuracy, analytical timeliness and reliability of investment implications.

6.8 Credit Research Analyst

Mandate: Evaluate issuer creditworthiness and identify opportunities and risks in debt securities.

Responsibilities: Analyse leverage, liquidity, cash flows, capital structure, debt covenants, recovery prospects and refinancing requirements; develop credit scenarios; and assess the adequacy of credit spreads.

Deliverables: Credit research reports, issuer risk assessments and credit valuation models.

KPIs: Credit forecast accuracy, identification of deterioration, investment contribution and risk assessment quality.

6.9 Event Driven Researcher

Mandate: Research investment opportunities associated with identifiable corporate or market events.

Responsibilities: Analyse mergers, restructurings, tender offers, regulatory decisions and other approved event categories; assess completion risks and expected timelines; construct probability-weighted valuation scenarios; and evaluate execution and liquidity constraints.

Deliverables: Event-driven investment memoranda, scenario models and event monitoring reports.

KPIs: Scenario calibration, research accuracy, event coverage and realised investment contribution.

7. Data Organisation

Division mandate: Acquire, validate, standardise and maintain the information infrastructure used by the firm's research, trading and risk management systems.

Division head: Chief Data Officer.

The division must preserve data provenance and the distinction between when an event occurred, when the information was published and when it became available to the firm's systems.

This is essential for preventing historically inaccurate research and backtests.

7.1 Data Acquisition Agent

Mandate: Acquire approved financial and alternative datasets.

Responsibilities: Retrieve data through authorised APIs, vendor feeds and public sources; enforce acquisition schedules; validate source availability; record collection timestamps; and identify missing or interrupted data feeds.

Deliverables: Ingestion logs, raw datasets and acquisition status reports.

KPIs: Acquisition completeness, delivery latency, source reliability and error frequency.

7.2 Market Data Engineer

Mandate: Build and maintain real-time and historical financial market data pipelines.

Responsibilities: Integrate exchange and vendor feeds; process market data messages; manage timestamp synchronisation; implement data transformations; and provide stable interfaces for research and production systems.

Deliverables: Market data pipelines, feed documentation and infrastructure performance reports.

KPIs: Data latency, message-loss frequency, pipeline availability and ingestion accuracy.

7.3 Reference Data Manager

Mandate: Maintain the firm's authoritative security and instrument reference database.

Responsibilities: Manage instrument identifiers, exchange codes, currency definitions, trading calendars, contract specifications and issuer relationships; reconcile conflicting vendor records; and maintain historical instrument mappings.

Deliverables: Reference data master, security mapping tables and data quality reports.

KPIs: Instrument identification accuracy, coverage completeness and resolution of reference data exceptions.

7.4 Corporate Actions Manager

Mandate: Maintain authoritative corporate-action data for portfolio accounting, research and trading systems.

Responsibilities: Collect and reconcile dividend, split, merger, rights issue and restructuring information; maintain effective dates and election deadlines; coordinate security master updates; and ensure appropriate adjustments to historical market data.

Deliverables: Corporate-action database, adjustment records and event processing instructions.

KPIs: Event accuracy, processing timeliness and completeness of corporate-action adjustments.

7.5 Feature Store Manager

Mandate: Maintain a controlled repository of research and production model features.

Responsibilities: Standardise feature definitions; manage feature versions, timestamps and lineage; establish access permissions; monitor feature consistency; and maintain alignment between historical and production calculations.

Deliverables: Feature catalogue, versioned feature datasets and feature consistency reports.

KPIs: Feature availability, reproducibility, freshness and historical-production consistency.

7.6 Data QA Agent

Mandate: Perform automated quality assurance on incoming and transformed datasets.

Responsibilities: Detect missing observations, duplicate records, abnormal values, timestamp inconsistencies and schema changes; compare data against expected specifications; and quarantine records failing approved quality thresholds.

Deliverables: Quality assurance reports, anomaly alerts and data exception records.

KPIs: Defect detection, false-positive frequency, data coverage and remediation timeliness.

7.7 Data Integrity Auditor

Mandate: Independently assess the integrity of data used in investment and operational systems.

Responsibilities: Audit source lineage, transformation procedures, access controls and historical revisions; investigate suspected data contamination; assess point-in-time correctness; and review compliance with data governance policies.

Deliverables: Independent data audit reports, integrity findings and remediation recommendations.

KPIs: Audit coverage, identification of material defects and remediation verification.

7.8 Alternative Data Pipeline Agent

Mandate: Process alternative datasets into structured and research-ready information.

Responsibilities: Integrate approved alternative data sources; perform cleaning and entity resolution; manage publication lags and geographical coverage; document sampling limitations; and deliver appropriately timestamped features.

Deliverables: Alternative data pipelines, transformation records and dataset documentation.

KPIs: Pipeline reliability, entity resolution accuracy, data freshness and provenance completeness.

7.9 Data Vendor Manager

Mandate: Manage relationships and commercial agreements with market data and alternative data providers.

Responsibilities: Evaluate vendors, negotiate licensing terms, monitor contractual obligations, assess data quality and coordinate service escalations; maintain records of usage restrictions and permitted redistribution.

Deliverables: Vendor evaluations, licensing register and service performance reviews.

KPIs: Vendor reliability, cost efficiency, licensing compliance and service issue resolution.

7.10 Historical Database Manager

Mandate: Maintain the firm's historical financial data repositories.

Responsibilities: Establish storage architecture and retention policies; manage historical revisions, data partitioning and backup procedures; preserve historical security identifiers; and provide reproducible access to archived market information.

Deliverables: Historical database, backup records and database integrity reports.

KPIs: Query reliability, archival completeness, recovery capability and data availability.

7.11 Tick Data Specialist

Mandate: Maintain and validate high-frequency trade and quote datasets.

Responsibilities: Process tick-level market data; reconcile exchange timestamps and sequence numbers; reconstruct order-book states where supported; identify abnormal market messages; and provide validated data for microstructure research and execution simulation.

8. Trading Division

Division mandate: Implement approved investment strategies through controlled, efficient and compliant market execution.

Division head: Head of Trading, reporting to the CIO.

Trading personnel and agents must distinguish investment decision-making authority from order execution authority.

The firm must implement appropriate pre-trade controls, monitoring and emergency procedures for automated order submission.

Where US broker-dealer market-access requirements apply, SEC Rule 15c3-5 establishes specific obligations relating to financial exposure limits, erroneous orders and regulatory controls.

8.1 Quantitative Trader

Mandate: Translate validated quantitative investment signals into executable trading decisions.

Responsibilities: Evaluate real-time model outputs, market conditions and available liquidity; implement approved position-sizing rules; monitor trading costs and strategy performance; and investigate deviations between theoretical and realised execution.

Deliverables: Trading activity reports, realised strategy performance analyses and execution recommendations.

KPIs: Net trading P&L, execution quality, risk compliance and operational reliability.

8.2 Systematic Trader

Mandate: Operate production trading strategies governed by predefined systematic rules.

Responsibilities: Supervise automated trading systems; verify signal-to-order translation; monitor strategy parameters, positions and order states; investigate anomalous behaviour; and initiate approved suspension or recovery procedures.

Deliverables: Production strategy logs, trading exception reports and systematic performance summaries.

KPIs: Strategy availability, implementation accuracy, slippage and adherence to approved trading rules.

8.3 Futures Trader

Mandate: Execute and manage approved futures transactions.

Responsibilities: Monitor contract liquidity and exchange specifications; execute futures orders; manage expiry and roll requirements; monitor margin implications; and coordinate with portfolio managers and treasury.

Deliverables: Futures execution records, roll reports and contract exposure reconciliations.

KPIs: Execution slippage, rollover efficiency, order accuracy and margin compliance.

8.4 Equity Trader

Mandate: Execute equity transactions in accordance with approved portfolio instructions.

Responsibilities: Assess market liquidity, order-book conditions and execution venues; select appropriate execution algorithms; manage large or illiquid orders; and monitor market impact and transaction costs.

Deliverables: Equity execution reports, order records and transaction-cost analyses.

KPIs: Execution shortfall, fill quality, market impact and order completion.

8.5 ETF Trader

Mandate: Execute ETF transactions efficiently across approved trading venues.

Responsibilities: Evaluate displayed liquidity, underlying basket liquidity, premiums and discounts to indicative value; coordinate ETF creation-redemption activity where authorised; and manage market impact.

Deliverables: ETF execution records, liquidity assessments and execution quality reports.

KPIs: Execution cost, fill reliability, premium-discount execution efficiency and operational accuracy.

8.6 Options Trader

Mandate: Execute options transactions while managing instrument-specific market and operational risks.

Responsibilities: Evaluate option-chain liquidity, implied volatility, Greeks and bid-ask spreads; execute approved options strategies; coordinate hedging; and monitor expiry, assignment and exercise exposures.

Deliverables: Options execution records, Greeks exposure reports and hedge execution summaries.

KPIs: Execution quality, hedge slippage, order accuracy and risk-limit compliance.

8.7 Market Making Trader

Mandate: Execute authorised liquidity-provision strategies within the firm's market-making business.

Responsibilities: Manage displayed bid and offer quotes, monitor inventory, adjust quoting behaviour to market conditions, execute inventory hedges and supervise automated quote generation.

Deliverables: Market-making trading reports, inventory analyses and quote performance records.

KPIs: Net trading P&L, realised spread capture, inventory risk and adverse-selection losses.

8.8 Statistical Arbitrage Trader

Mandate: Execute approved statistical arbitrage strategies while preserving their intended relative-value exposures.

Responsibilities: Coordinate linked orders, manage partial fills, monitor spread deviations, control unintended directional exposure and implement strategy-specific exit rules.

Deliverables: Statistical arbitrage execution reports, residual exposure analyses and trade-level P&L attribution.

KPIs: Spread capture, execution shortfall, hedge completeness and risk-adjusted trading performance.

8.9 Volatility Trader

Mandate: Execute volatility-related strategies and manage the trading implications of changing option sensitivities.

Responsibilities: Monitor implied and realised volatility, execute volatility positions, coordinate delta hedging, manage exposure during market stress and assess transaction costs associated with hedge adjustments.

Deliverables: Volatility trading reports, Greeks exposure records and hedge cost analyses.

KPIs: Net volatility trading P&L, hedge efficiency, liquidity management and risk compliance.

8.10 Execution Trader

Mandate: Execute investment orders at favourable achievable prices while controlling market impact and operational risk.

Responsibilities: Select execution schedules and algorithms; assess market depth and urgency; manage order fragmentation; monitor fill quality; and coordinate with portfolio management and execution analytics teams.

Deliverables: Execution reports, order-level performance analyses and trading cost reviews.

KPIs: Implementation shortfall, fill rate, realised market impact and execution reliability.

8.11 Smart Order Router

Mandate: Route approved orders among eligible execution venues using predefined optimisation and compliance rules.

Responsibilities: Evaluate available liquidity, transaction fees, latency and venue characteristics; select order destinations; monitor order acknowledgements and fill states; and enforce applicable routing restrictions and risk controls.

Deliverables: Routing decisions, venue performance statistics and order audit logs.

KPIs: Routing latency, execution quality, fill probability and order rejection frequency.

8.12 Liquidity Manager

Mandate: Maintain sufficient market and funding liquidity to support trading and portfolio requirements.

Responsibilities: Monitor available market depth, liquidation capacity, trading concentration and liquidity demands; coordinate funding requirements with treasury; and recommend changes to execution schedules or portfolio liquidity reserves.

Deliverables: Liquidity dashboards, funding requirement forecasts and liquidity stress reports.

KPIs: Liquidity adequacy, funding efficiency, liquidation cost and settlement readiness.

8.13 Trade Surveillance Agent

Mandate: Monitor trading activity for potential market abuse, unauthorised transactions and operational anomalies.

Responsibilities: Analyse order submissions, cancellations, executions and position changes; identify suspicious trading patterns; generate explainable alerts; and preserve evidence for compliance investigation.

Deliverables: Surveillance alerts, trade exception reports and investigation records.

KPIs: Alert precision, detection coverage, escalation timeliness and completeness of surveillance records.

Independence requirement: Although this agent processes Trading Division data, investigation and final surveillance oversight must remain under the Compliance Division.

9. Market-Making Division
Citadel Securities-Inspired Market-Making Operating Model

Division mandate: Provide liquidity through authorised principal trading, manage market-making inventory and operate automated quotation and execution infrastructure.

Division head: Head of Market Making.

The following structure is inspired by the functional requirements of electronic liquidity provision. It is not a representation of Citadel Securities' actual internal organisation.

A market-making business requires capabilities beyond directional investment research, including exchange connectivity, inventory management, quotation optimisation, adverse-selection modelling and continuous trading risk controls.

The FCA's review of principal algorithmic trading firms specifically discusses the contribution of such firms to liquidity and price formation, alongside the importance of effective risk and compliance controls.

9.1 Exchange Connectivity Manager

Mandate: Maintain the technical and operational infrastructure connecting the trading platform to authorised exchanges and trading venues.

Responsibilities: Manage exchange sessions, market data subscriptions, order-entry gateways and protocol compatibility; supervise connectivity testing; monitor message throughput; and coordinate recovery from exchange or network failures.

Deliverables: Exchange connectivity architecture, connectivity health reports and incident records.

KPIs: Connection availability, message latency, order rejection frequency and recovery time.

9.2 Market Maker

Mandate: Provide executable bid and offer quotations while managing the risks associated with principal trading.

Responsibilities: Determine quote placement within authorised parameters; manage inventory accumulation; respond to changing liquidity and volatility; coordinate hedging; and monitor realised spread capture.

Deliverables: Market-making positions, quoting activity records and inventory performance reports.

KPIs: Net trading P&L, spread capture, inventory risk, quote availability and adverse-selection costs.

9.3 Bid Offer Optimiser

Mandate: Optimise market-making bid and offer prices conditional on market conditions and inventory.

Responsibilities: Estimate fair value, fill probability, adverse-selection risk and inventory costs; calculate permissible quote adjustments; incorporate exchange fees; and evaluate the impact of alternative spread settings.

Deliverables: Quote optimisation models, spread recommendations and execution simulation results.

KPIs: Risk-adjusted spread capture, quote competitiveness, fill quality and inventory-adjusted profitability.

9.4 Inventory Manager

Mandate: Control market-making inventory and prevent excessive accumulation of directional or correlated exposures.

Responsibilities: Monitor net and gross positions; calculate inventory concentration; coordinate hedges and inventory-reduction orders; and recommend quote skew adjustments when inventory approaches approved limits.

Deliverables: Real-time inventory dashboard, inventory risk reports and hedge recommendations.

KPIs: Inventory utilisation, concentration, hedge efficiency and inventory-related losses.

9.5 Quote Manager

Mandate: Maintain the lifecycle of automated market-making quotations.

Responsibilities: Generate, update and cancel quotes according to approved pricing inputs; monitor quote acknowledgements; enforce quote validity and order-state consistency; and prevent duplicate or stale quotations.

Deliverables: Quote lifecycle records, quotation status dashboards and exception alerts.

KPIs: Quote accuracy, update latency, stale-quote frequency and order-state consistency.

9.6 Spread Controller

Mandate: Adjust quoted spreads to reflect changing market liquidity, volatility and trading risk.

Responsibilities: Estimate the minimum economically acceptable spread; incorporate fees, inventory costs and adverse-selection expectations; apply approved spread floors and ceilings; and monitor realised outcomes.

Deliverables: Spread control parameters, spread adjustment records and realised spread analyses.

KPIs: Spread capture, fill probability, quotation competitiveness and adverse-selection-adjusted profitability.

9.7 Liquidity Forecasting Agent

Mandate: Forecast short-horizon changes in market liquidity.

Responsibilities: Analyse order-book depth, order arrival rates, trade intensity, cancellations and historical intraday patterns; forecast available liquidity; and identify conditions likely to impair execution or inventory liquidation.

Deliverables: Liquidity forecasts, order-book depth projections and liquidity deterioration alerts.

KPIs: Forecast calibration, liquidity prediction error and timeliness of alerts.

9.8 Adverse Selection Detector

Mandate: Identify circumstances in which passive quotations are disproportionately likely to execute before unfavourable price movements.

Responsibilities: Analyse order-flow imbalance, trade direction, short-term price dynamics and market data updates; estimate expected post-fill price movement; and recommend quotation adjustments or temporary suspensions.

Deliverables: Adverse-selection estimates, risk alerts and quotation adjustment recommendations.

KPIs: Post-fill markout accuracy, adverse-selection cost reduction and false-alert frequency.

9.9 Hedging Agent

Mandate: Maintain market-making exposures within approved limits through authorised hedging transactions.

Responsibilities: Calculate net inventory and factor sensitivities; identify appropriate hedge instruments; generate hedge recommendations or approved orders; monitor hedge execution; and identify residual exposures.

Deliverables: Hedge orders, hedge effectiveness reports and residual risk analyses.

KPIs: Hedge effectiveness, transaction costs, residual exposure and risk-limit compliance.

9.10 ETF Creation Redemption Agent

Mandate: Coordinate the operational and analytical requirements of authorised ETF creation and redemption activity.

Responsibilities: Evaluate ETF and underlying basket values; calculate transaction and financing costs; identify economically feasible creation-redemption opportunities; and generate instructions for review by authorised personnel.

Deliverables: ETF basket calculations, creation-redemption recommendations and settlement instructions.

KPIs: Basket valuation accuracy, operational timeliness, transaction cost and settlement completion.

Important restriction: Actual creation-redemption orders must be submitted through appropriately authorised entities and processes.

9.11 AP Relationship Manager

Mandate: Manage relationships with authorised participants and ETF ecosystem counterparties.

Responsibilities: Coordinate creation-redemption workflows; monitor contractual obligations and operational service levels; resolve basket settlement exceptions; and manage commercial and operational communications with counterparties.

Deliverables: Counterparty relationship records, AP service reviews and ETF transaction coordination reports.

KPIs: Counterparty reliability, settlement success, operational responsiveness and transaction efficiency.

9.12 Market Impact Forecaster

Mandate: Estimate the expected price impact associated with proposed trading activity.

Responsibilities: Analyse order size, execution horizon, market depth, volatility and participation rates; develop temporary and persistent impact models; and support order scheduling and hedge execution decisions.

Deliverables: Market impact forecasts, execution cost curves and model validation reports.

KPIs: Forecast accuracy, stability across liquidity conditions and execution cost estimation quality.

9.13 Execution Quality Analyst

Mandate: Independently assess the quality of completed trading activity.

Responsibilities: Compare executions against suitable market benchmarks; analyse fill rates, slippage, venue performance and order handling; identify systematic execution deficiencies; and recommend operational improvements.

Deliverables: Execution quality scorecards, venue analyses and execution improvement recommendations.

KPIs: Analytical accuracy, identification of material execution deficiencies and verified cost improvements.

9.14 Transaction Cost Analyst

Mandate: Quantify the direct and indirect costs associated with trading activity.

Responsibilities: Decompose execution costs into commissions, fees, bid-ask spread, market impact, financing and opportunity costs; compare expected and realised costs; and maintain transaction-cost assumptions used in research and portfolio management.

Deliverables: Transaction cost models, trade-level cost attribution and strategy cost reports.

KPIs: Cost estimation accuracy, analytical coverage and consistency between expected and realised trading costs.

10. Risk Division

Division mandate: Independently identify, quantify, monitor and control risks arising from the firm's investment, trading and operational activities.

Division head: Chief Risk Officer.

Risk management must evaluate more than historical volatility.

The framework must account for leverage, nonlinear exposures, correlation instability, liquidity constraints, operational failure and extreme but plausible market conditions.

Risk personnel must have direct escalation channels to the CRO and relevant governing committees.

10.1 Enterprise Risk Manager

Mandate: Coordinate the firm's integrated enterprise risk management framework.

Responsibilities: Maintain the enterprise risk register; evaluate financial, operational, technological and third-party risks; coordinate risk assessments; monitor remediation actions; and report material risk concentrations to senior management.

Deliverables: Enterprise risk reports, risk registers and mitigation plans.

KPIs: Risk coverage, escalation timeliness and remediation effectiveness.

10.2 Portfolio Risk Quant

Mandate: Develop and maintain quantitative models measuring portfolio risk.

Responsibilities: Estimate volatility, covariance, factor sensitivities and tail risk; develop portfolio risk analytics; validate model inputs and assumptions; and evaluate risk concentrations across strategies and asset classes.

Deliverables: Portfolio risk models, risk decomposition reports and risk-model documentation.

KPIs: Risk measurement reliability, model accuracy and timely identification of material portfolio exposures.

10.3 VaR Engine

Mandate: Calculate Value at Risk using approved methodologies.

Responsibilities: Implement historical simulation, parametric and Monte Carlo VaR where appropriate; calculate risk over defined horizons and confidence levels; perform VaR backtesting; and investigate breaches and model limitations.

Deliverables: VaR calculations, backtesting reports and risk-limit alerts.

KPIs: Calculation accuracy, model coverage, backtesting performance and reporting timeliness.

10.4 Stress Testing Agent

Mandate: Estimate portfolio losses under severe hypothetical or historical market conditions.

Responsibilities: Apply approved stress scenarios to positions; calculate portfolio and strategy-level impacts; incorporate nonlinear sensitivities where relevant; and identify exposure concentrations.

Deliverables: Stress-loss reports, scenario sensitivity matrices and risk alerts.

KPIs: Scenario coverage, calculation reliability and timely identification of stress vulnerabilities.

10.5 Scenario Analysis Agent

Mandate: Evaluate portfolio performance under alternative macroeconomic and market scenarios.

Responsibilities: Translate changes in interest rates, inflation, FX, equity prices, volatility and correlations into portfolio impacts; develop internally consistent scenarios; and assess the consequences of alternative investment decisions.

Deliverables: Scenario analysis reports and portfolio sensitivity assessments.

KPIs: Scenario consistency, model reliability and analytical usefulness.

10.6 Drawdown Monitoring Agent

Mandate: Monitor realised portfolio and strategy losses relative to previous performance peaks.

Responsibilities: Calculate rolling and peak-to-trough drawdowns; monitor approved drawdown thresholds; distinguish strategy-specific losses from market-wide losses; and escalate breaches.

Deliverables: Drawdown dashboards, breach alerts and recovery analyses.

KPIs: Monitoring accuracy, alert latency and adherence to drawdown escalation procedures.

10.7 Exposure Manager

Mandate: Monitor and control aggregate portfolio exposures.

Responsibilities: Consolidate gross and net exposures; monitor leverage, asset-class concentration, geographical exposure and counterparty dependencies; and compare portfolio positions against approved limits.

Deliverables: Consolidated exposure reports, concentration dashboards and limit breach notifications.

KPIs: Exposure reconciliation accuracy, limit compliance and reporting latency.

10.8 Factor Exposure Agent

Mandate: Estimate portfolio sensitivities to systematic investment factors.

Responsibilities: Calculate exposures to market beta, sectors, styles, duration, credit spreads, currencies and other relevant factors; identify hidden common exposures across strategies; and monitor factor concentration.

Deliverables: Factor exposure reports, risk attribution and concentration alerts.

KPIs: Exposure estimation accuracy, model coverage and identification of unintended factor risks.

10.9 Tail Risk Manager

Mandate: Evaluate and manage exposure to extreme adverse market outcomes.

Responsibilities: Develop expected shortfall and tail-dependence analytics; assess nonlinear derivatives exposure; evaluate downside hedging; examine liquidity under stress; and recommend tail-risk mitigation measures.

Deliverables: Tail-risk assessments, downside scenario reports and hedging recommendations.

KPIs: Risk coverage, hedge effectiveness and identification of material extreme-loss exposures.

10.10 Correlation Monitoring Agent

Mandate: Monitor changing relationships among portfolio assets and trading strategies.

Responsibilities: Estimate rolling and regime-conditioned correlations; detect correlation instability; identify common-factor concentration; and evaluate the consequences of simultaneous strategy losses.

Deliverables: Correlation matrices, dependency-change alerts and diversification assessments.

KPIs: Detection of material correlation changes, estimation reliability and reporting timeliness.

10.11 Liquidity Risk Agent

Mandate: Measure portfolio liquidity and potential losses associated with constrained liquidation.

Responsibilities: Estimate time to liquidate positions, market depth, liquidation costs, funding requirements and liquidity concentration; perform stressed liquidity analyses; and monitor portfolio redemption or margin-related liquidity demands.

Deliverables: Liquidity risk reports, liquidation horizon estimates and stress liquidity projections.

KPIs: Liquidity forecast accuracy, risk identification and timely escalation.

10.12 Counterparty Risk Agent

Mandate: Evaluate risks arising from the failure or deterioration of trading, financing and custody counterparties.

Responsibilities: Monitor exposures to brokers, clearing members, custodians, exchanges and OTC counterparties; assess collateral arrangements and concentration; evaluate counterparty credit developments; and recommend exposure adjustments.

Deliverables: Counterparty exposure reports, credit monitoring alerts and concentration assessments.

KPIs: Exposure accuracy, counterparty coverage and escalation timeliness.

10.13 Risk Reporting Agent

Mandate: Produce consistent, accurate and timely reports of portfolio and enterprise risk.

Responsibilities: Consolidate risk metrics from approved systems; reconcile exposures; prepare daily and periodic risk reports; identify material changes; and distribute reports according to approved escalation rules.

Deliverables: Daily risk dashboards, committee reports and risk exception summaries.

KPIs: Reporting accuracy, completeness, timeliness and auditability.

11. Strategy Validation Division

Division mandate: Independently challenge investment research, backtesting methodologies and model assumptions before strategies are approved for production deployment.

Division head: Head of Independent Strategy Validation.

The division must be organisationally independent from the teams responsible for originating and developing investment strategies.

The firm's validation framework should distinguish conceptual soundness, implementation correctness, empirical robustness and ongoing performance monitoring.

These categories are consistent with the broader model-risk principles described in the Federal Reserve's SR 11-7 guidance.

11.1 Independent Backtest Agent

Mandate: Reconstruct proposed investment strategies using an independently maintained backtesting environment.

Responsibilities: Verify signal logic, portfolio accounting, transaction-cost assumptions, order execution, financing costs and corporate-action treatment; reproduce reported results; and investigate differences between research and independent backtests.

Deliverables: Independent backtest reports, discrepancy analyses and reproducibility records.

KPIs: Reproducibility, error detection, model coverage and independent implementation accuracy.

11.2 Strategy Challenger

Mandate: Critically evaluate the economic and empirical justification for proposed trading strategies.

Responsibilities: Develop competing hypotheses; test alternative parameterisations; investigate whether results depend on narrow assumptions; assess economic plausibility; and identify conditions that could invalidate the proposed strategy.

Deliverables: Strategy challenge memorandum, competing-model results and research revision recommendations.

KPIs: Quality of independent challenge, identification of material weaknesses and completeness of alternative explanations.

11.3 Research Validation Agent

Mandate: Verify the methodological integrity and reproducibility of quantitative research.

Responsibilities: Review research hypotheses, data selection, statistical calculations and model documentation; reproduce material findings; confirm temporal validity; and examine the relationship between reported results and supporting evidence.

Deliverables: Research validation reports, methodological exception logs and validation recommendations.

KPIs: Validation coverage, reproducibility and identification of material research defects.

11.4 Walk Forward Validation Agent

Mandate: Evaluate strategy performance using sequential training and testing procedures.

Responsibilities: Construct rolling or expanding training windows; retrain models according to documented schedules; preserve chronological information boundaries; simulate parameter updates; and aggregate genuinely out-of-sample results.

Deliverables: Walk-forward performance reports, model stability analyses and parameter evolution records.

KPIs: Validation integrity, model stability and reproducibility of out-of-sample performance.

11.5 Overfitting Detector

Mandate: Identify strategies whose historical performance may depend excessively on model complexity or repeated optimisation.

Responsibilities: Evaluate model complexity, parameter sensitivity, selection bias and multiple hypothesis testing; compare in-sample and out-of-sample performance; and implement appropriate statistical diagnostics.

Deliverables: Overfitting risk reports, sensitivity analyses and model complexity assessments.

KPIs: Detection of fragile research, methodological coverage and effectiveness of overfitting diagnostics.

11.6 Data Leakage Detector

Mandate: Identify inappropriate use of information unavailable at the time of a simulated trading decision.

Responsibilities: Audit data timestamps, train-test boundaries, preprocessing, feature construction and fundamental-data publication lags; inspect target leakage; and verify that historical research uses appropriate point-in-time datasets.

Deliverables: Leakage audit reports, timestamp verification records and remediation recommendations.

KPIs: Leakage detection, temporal audit coverage and resolution of identified defects.

11.7 Robustness Tester

Mandate: Evaluate strategy performance under alternative assumptions and operating conditions.

Responsibilities: Test parameter perturbations, transaction-cost increases, execution delays, missing data, changes in universe composition and adverse market regimes; identify fragile dependencies; and assess strategy capacity.

Deliverables: Robustness test matrix, sensitivity reports and implementation feasibility assessments.

KPIs: Test coverage, reproducibility and identification of material strategy vulnerabilities.

11.8 Statistical Significance Validator

Mandate: Assess whether reported research findings are adequately supported by statistical evidence.

Responsibilities: Evaluate hypothesis testing, confidence intervals, dependence assumptions and multiple-testing adjustments; investigate data snooping; apply appropriate bootstrap or resampling methods; and distinguish economic significance from statistical significance.

Deliverables: Statistical validation reports, uncertainty estimates and hypothesis testing documentation.

KPIs: Methodological accuracy, statistical coverage and reproducibility.

11.9 Model Approval Agent

Mandate: Consolidate independent validation findings and prepare formal model approval recommendations.

Responsibilities: Verify completion of required validation procedures; assess unresolved findings; classify model risks; confirm documentation completeness; and prepare approval, conditional approval or rejection recommendations for authorised decision-makers.

Deliverables: Model approval dossiers, outstanding issue registers and approval recommendations.

KPIs: Review completeness, policy adherence and accuracy of approval records.

Authority: Advisory. Final production approval must follow the firm's designated governance process.

11.10 Model Governance Agent

Mandate: Maintain the lifecycle governance framework for research and production models.

Responsibilities: Maintain the model inventory; record ownership, versions, approvals and limitations; monitor review schedules; track model changes and validation findings; and ensure that retired models are removed from authorised production use.

Deliverables: Model inventory, governance reports, validation schedules and change-control records.

KPIs: Inventory completeness, review compliance, unresolved findings and governance traceability.

Division head: Head of AI Systems and Agentic Infrastructure.

The AI division provides orchestration and infrastructure rather than replacing the accountability of investment, risk, compliance and executive management.

Its operating framework should cover the identification, measurement, management and governance of AI-specific risks.

The NIST AI Risk Management Framework provides a relevant reference for structuring these activities across the AI lifecycle.

13.4 Agent Performance Manager

13.6 Agent Memory Manager

13.7 Prompt Engineer

13.8 Tool Verification Agent

13.9 Hallucination Detection Agent

Deliverables: Factual verification reports, unsupported-claim alerts and evidence assessments.

13.10 AI Governance Agent

Deliverables: AI governance reports, agent approval records and governance exception registers.

13.11 Model Selection Agent

13.12 Knowledge Graph Manager

14. Compliance and Legal Division

Division mandate: Ensure that the firm's business activities comply with applicable laws, regulations, contractual obligations and internal conduct standards.

Division head: Chief Compliance Officer.

Legal and compliance responsibilities must be allocated according to the firm's actual operating jurisdictions, entity structure, regulatory permissions and investment products.

Automated monitoring can support compliance functions, but legal judgements, regulatory accountability and formal compliance decisions must remain with appropriately authorised personnel.

14.1 Compliance Analyst

Mandate: Monitor the firm's adherence to applicable regulatory and internal compliance requirements.

Responsibilities: Conduct compliance testing; review trading and investment activities; investigate policy exceptions; maintain compliance records; and coordinate remediation with relevant business divisions.

Deliverables: Compliance monitoring reports, exception registers and investigation records.

KPIs: Monitoring coverage, review timeliness and remediation completion.

14.2 Regulatory Monitoring Agent

Deliverables: Regulatory change alerts, obligations register updates and regulatory impact summaries.

14.3 Trade Surveillance Agent

14.4 Market Abuse Detector

Deliverables: Market abuse alerts, supporting transaction analyses and investigation referrals.

15. Operations Division

Division head: Chief Operating Officer.

The division must maintain appropriate separation between trade execution, transaction processing, reconciliation and financial control.

15.1 Trade Operations Manager

15.3 Reconciliation Agent

15.4 Cash Management Agent

KPIs: Accounting accuracy, reconciliation completeness and reporting timeliness.

15.7 PnL Controller

Mandate: Independently verify and explain trading and investment profit and loss.

16. Cybersecurity Division

Division mandate: Protect the confidentiality, integrity and availability of the firm's information systems, trading infrastructure, investment data and operational processes.

Division head: Chief Information Security Officer or designated Head of Cybersecurity.

The division should establish controls across security governance, asset identification, protection, detection, incident response and recovery.

These functions correspond to the six components of NIST's Cybersecurity Framework 2.0.

16.1 Security Operations Centre Agent

Mandate: Continuously monitor the firm's systems for potential cybersecurity incidents.

Responsibilities: Analyse security logs and system alerts; detect anomalous activity; correlate security events; create investigation cases; and escalate potential incidents according to severity.

Deliverables: Security alerts, incident cases and operational security reports.

KPIs: Detection latency, alert quality, monitoring coverage and escalation timeliness.

16.2 Threat Intelligence Agent

Mandate: Identify external cyber threats relevant to the firm's operating environment.

Responsibilities: Monitor approved threat intelligence sources; assess emerging attack techniques; identify threats targeting financial infrastructure; and provide actionable intelligence to security operations and engineering teams.

Deliverables: Threat intelligence briefs, threat indicator records and security recommendations.

KPIs: Intelligence relevance, alert timeliness and actionable threat coverage.

16.3 Vulnerability Scanner

Mandate: Identify security vulnerabilities affecting the firm's technology infrastructure.

Responsibilities: Conduct authorised automated vulnerability assessments; maintain vulnerability inventories; classify findings by severity and exploitability; and monitor remediation progress.

Deliverables: Vulnerability reports, prioritised remediation recommendations and verification results.

KPIs: Asset coverage, detection accuracy and vulnerability remediation time.

16.4 Incident Response Agent

Mandate: Support the identification, containment and recovery of cybersecurity incidents.

Responsibilities: Analyse incident evidence; execute explicitly authorised containment actions; coordinate incident workflows; maintain incident timelines; and support post-incident analysis.

Deliverables: Incident response records, containment recommendations and recovery reports.

KPIs: Incident response time, containment effectiveness, evidence preservation and recovery performance.

Authority: High-impact actions affecting production trading or critical infrastructure require designated human approval unless a specific emergency action has been pre-authorised.

16.5 Penetration Testing Agent

Mandate: Conduct controlled security assessments of approved systems.

Responsibilities: Evaluate attack surfaces; test security configurations; identify exploitable weaknesses; verify security controls; and document findings for remediation.

Deliverables: Penetration testing reports, vulnerability evidence and remediation recommendations.

KPIs: Assessment coverage, finding accuracy and remediation verification.

Restriction: Testing must remain within explicitly authorised systems, scopes and operational windows.

16.6 Access Control Auditor

Mandate: Independently review identity and access management arrangements.

Responsibilities: Evaluate user and agent permissions; identify excessive privileges; monitor privileged access; assess segregation of duties; and verify the timely removal of obsolete access rights.

Deliverables: Access audit reports, privilege exception registers and remediation recommendations.

KPIs: Access review completeness, excessive privilege detection and remediation timeliness.

16.7 Secrets Management Agent

Mandate: Protect credentials, API keys, certificates and other sensitive authentication material.

Responsibilities: Maintain secure secrets storage; enforce access policies; monitor credential usage; coordinate credential rotation; and detect potentially exposed or improperly stored secrets.

Deliverables: Secrets inventory, rotation records and credential security alerts.

KPIs: Credential exposure prevention, rotation compliance and access-control integrity.

17. Executive Oversight Layer

Division mandate: Coordinate firm-wide decision-making, supervise cross-divisional governance and ensure that investment, risk, operational and technological decisions remain consistent with the firm's approved objectives.

Ultimate authority: Board of Directors and designated human executive committees.

The executive oversight agents described below support committee processes by preparing evidence, identifying inconsistencies and monitoring implementation.

They do not replace the accountability of duly appointed directors, executives or authorised investment decision-makers.

17.1 Chief of Staff Agent

Mandate: Coordinate strategic and operational workflows across the firm's executive management team and business divisions.

Responsibilities: Consolidate divisional reports; track strategic initiatives; identify unresolved cross-functional dependencies; maintain executive action registers; coordinate committee agendas; and monitor the implementation of approved decisions.

Deliverables: Executive briefings, strategic initiative dashboards, decision registers and cross-divisional status reports.

KPIs: Coordination timeliness, action completion, reporting accuracy and escalation effectiveness.

Authority: May assign and coordinate tasks within its approved mandate but cannot independently change investment risk limits or authorise trading.

17.2 Investment Committee Agent

Mandate: Support the formal evaluation and approval of proposed investment strategies.

Responsibilities: Assemble investment research, independent validation results, portfolio implications and risk assessments; assess whether required evidence is complete; identify unresolved disagreements; and prepare structured strategy approval recommendations.

Deliverables: Investment Committee dossiers, investment decision records and outstanding issue reports.

KPIs: Review completeness, decision traceability, adherence to approval procedures and quality of investment evidence.

Authority: Advisory. Formal approval must be exercised by the authorised Investment Committee or delegated human decision-maker.

17.3 Risk Committee Agent

Mandate: Support executive oversight of portfolio, strategy and enterprise risk.

Responsibilities: Consolidate independent risk assessments; monitor risk-limit proposals and breaches; review stress-testing results; identify unresolved risk concerns; and prepare recommendations for changes to the firm's risk framework.

Deliverables: Risk Committee reports, risk-limit review packages and material risk escalation records.

KPIs: Risk reporting completeness, escalation timeliness, analytical accuracy and governance traceability.

Authority: May trigger predefined risk alerts and authorised emergency controls but cannot unilaterally expand approved risk limits.

17.4 Capital Allocation Committee Agent

Mandate: Support the evaluation and allocation of investment capital among approved strategies and portfolios.

Responsibilities: Consolidate strategy performance, expected returns, drawdowns, capacity, correlation and liquidity information; develop allocation scenarios; assess the effect of proposed allocations on overall portfolio risk; and prepare capital allocation recommendations.

Deliverables: Capital allocation proposals, strategy capacity analyses and allocation decision records.

KPIs: Allocation analysis accuracy, capital utilisation, diversification analysis and decision documentation.

Authority: Advisory unless specific bounded allocation adjustments have been explicitly pre-authorised.

17.5 Governance Committee Agent

Mandate: Coordinate the governance process for material model, technology and operational changes.

Responsibilities: Verify that deployment proposals contain the required validation, security, compliance and operational evidence; maintain approval records; identify unresolved control findings; and monitor compliance with production change-management procedures.

Deliverables: Governance approval dossiers, deployment readiness assessments and governance exception reports.

KPIs: Governance review completeness, deployment compliance, documentation quality and unresolved control findings.

Authority: May reject incomplete deployment submissions or suspend an approval workflow, but final approval must follow the authorised governance process.

IMPLEMENTATION AND ACCOUNTABILITY NOTES
- These are proposed additional jobs or agent functions, not a claim that any named financial institution uses identical titles.
- Core means a defined function should exist in the proposed operating model, not necessarily a separate full-time employee. Conditional roles activate only if the stated activity, licence, product, client base or material exposure exists.
- Market making and asset management may require separate legal entities and regulatory permissions; do not assume one permission covers the other.
- Risk, compliance, internal audit and independent validation must not be made subordinate to investment P&L; the board retains accountable human oversight.
- AI agents may assist with analyses and bounded operations but may not self-approve strategy release, extend tool permissions, determine legal culpability, or override binding risk controls.
- The 2023 US SEC private-fund adviser rule package was vacated in June 2024; do not use it as a current universal obligation. Have jurisdiction-specific counsel confirm applicable requirements.

IMPLEMENTATION RULES
This expansion retains every original job description above. It adds new role profiles and explicit scope updates where a requested title duplicates or substantially overlaps an existing position. Titles are functions, not an assertion that one full-time employee is required per title.
Line 1: research, portfolio management, trading, data and technology operate and monitor systems. Line 2: independent risk, compliance and model challenge review them. Line 3: internal audit reports independently to the board audit committee.
Agents may research and perform bounded pre-authorised actions, but may not grant themselves privileges, increase capital/risk limits, approve their own model releases, determine compliance findings, or restart suspended strategies without designated authorisation.
Conditional product/market-making, prime-brokerage-adjacent and securities lending mandates apply only when the relevant legal entities, counterparties and licences are in place. The firm is not automatically a prime broker or market maker because these roles exist on an organisation chart.
Metrics such as live Sharpe must be calculated over specified windows with confidence intervals and after financing/fees; no single threshold alone is proof of permanent alpha decay.

38. Research and Alpha Generation — Additional Specialisations
Reporting and oversight: Quantitative Research Director / CIO

38.1 Research Synthesis Agent
Mandate: Identify new testable investment hypotheses by combining research produced by different teams.
Responsibilities: Index validated findings across sectors, datasets and horizons; extract complementary hypotheses, conflicting results and opportunities for signal combination; cite sources and record what has already been tested.
Deliverables: Cross-team synthesis memo, hypothesis backlog and supporting evidence graph.
KPIs: Accepted research hypotheses, provenance completeness and rate of independently reproducible findings.
Integration / overlap: Distinct from Research Librarian: synthesises hypotheses rather than only cataloguing material.

38.2 Alpha Decay Monitor
Mandate: Detect deterioration in the predictive and economic value of approved alpha signals.
Responsibilities: Track rolling net Sharpe and information coefficient alongside confidence intervals, turnover, costs and exposure changes; distinguish statistical noise from sustained decay; refer suspected degradation for independent review.
Deliverables: Alpha-decay dashboard, degradation alerts and diagnostic referrals.
KPIs: Detection delay, false-alert rate and accuracy of net-performance decomposition.
Integration / overlap: Feeds Live Strategy Monitoring; does not independently retire a strategy.

38.3 Crowding Analyst
Mandate: Estimate strategy crowding and the risk of correlated exits by other investors.
Responsibilities: Combine public factor co-movement, financing and borrow measures, flow proxies and liquidity observations; stress crowded liquidation; state clearly when third-party positions are unobservable.
Deliverables: Crowding research note, uncertainty-qualified crowding measures and unwind scenarios.
KPIs: Measurement stability, explanatory power and timely identification of correlated drawdowns.
Integration / overlap: Separate from Portfolio Crowding Manager, who manages own-firm allocations.

38.4 Capacity Researcher
Mandate: Estimate research-stage scaling limits before a strategy is allocated capital.
Responsibilities: Model net alpha against deployed assets, market depth, turnover, slippage, borrow and cross-strategy overlap; produce sensitivity to deteriorating liquidity and market impact.
Deliverables: Research capacity curve, provisional strategy-size ceiling and assumptions register.
KPIs: Out-of-sample capacity calibration and accuracy of expected-versus-realised impact.
Integration / overlap: Portfolio Capacity Manager (19.14) owns live portfolio-level deployment caps.

38.5 Regime Forecasting Specialist
Mandate: Forecast probabilistic changes in market state without treating latent state labels as observed facts.
Responsibilities: Estimate regime transition probabilities and structural breaks with information available at the forecast date; test predictive calibration and stability; assess incremental value against simple alternatives.
Deliverables: Regime probability forecast, transition diagnostics and forecast uncertainty report.
KPIs: Calibration, out-of-sample log score and incremental performance net of switching costs.
Integration / overlap: Extends Regime Detection Researcher (5.9), which principally identifies and interprets states.

38.6 Cross-Asset Signal Researcher
Mandate: Discover testable predictive relationships among equities, rates, FX, commodities and authorised digital assets.
Responsibilities: Examine lead-lag dependence, macro transmission, common factor exposures and nonstationarity; test hypotheses point-in-time and across regimes; distinguish co-movement from tradeable predictive advantage.
Deliverables: Cross-asset feature definitions, hypothesis report and out-of-sample validation request.
KPIs: Incremental net information ratio, stability and source-timestamp integrity.
Integration / overlap: Coordinates with Macro Research Analyst and Signal Combination Researcher.

51. EXECUTIVE LEADERSHIP — SCIENCE AND ECONOMIC RESEARCH
Reporting and oversight: CEO / CIO for investment-research priorities; CDO remains data owner; CTO remains technology owner; CRO and independent validation retain challenge and veto powers within their delegated mandates.

51.1 Chief Data Scientist
Staffing applicability: Core when a multi-team machine-learning research and production ecosystem is material; otherwise a designated senior ML lead may hold this responsibility.
Reports to: CIO for investment-model research priorities; collaborates with CTO on production architecture and with CDO on enterprise data standards; independent model risk and validation report separately.
Role purpose: Provide accountable first-line scientific leadership for the firm's statistical learning and machine-learning model ecosystem, from common research standards through reproducible production hand-off.
Principal responsibilities:
- Define the ML research roadmap across alpha forecasting, portfolio construction, execution analytics and permitted AI applications, aligned to the firm's investment mandate and research budget.
- Set cross-model development standards for experiment tracking, temporal validation, reproducibility, model documentation, uncertainty calibration, interpretability and benchmark comparisons.
- Coordinate feature requirements and approved reuse with the CDO, Feature Store Manager and research teams without assuming ownership of source licensing, data governance or enterprise data acquisition.
- Establish model-family inventories and shared evaluation protocols; identify duplicated work, fragile assumptions, common data dependencies and cross-model failure modes.
- Sponsor the technical transition of validated ML research into production with Quant Developers, Research Platform Engineers, CTO and model owners; ensure approved features and model versions are immutable and recoverable.
- Monitor programme-level model effectiveness and research quality; commission investigation of observed drift without authorising unilateral model retraining, risk-limit changes or production releases.
- Develop researchers' competence, peer-review standards and responsible computing practices; coordinate material model decisions with the formal Model Approval Board.
Required deliverables: Annual ML roadmap; first-line model development standards; cross-model evaluation suite; model-family and dependency map; ML production hand-off checklist; quarterly model-ecosystem health review.
Performance indicators: Reproducibility of ML experiments; independent validation acceptance rate; deployment quality; incremental net value against simple baselines; training/production feature consistency; unresolved model-risk findings and compute efficiency (reported separately, not traded against control integrity).
Qualifications / expertise: Advanced applied statistics and machine learning, financial time-series methods, Python-based research engineering, MLOps and experience leading multidisciplinary scientific teams.
Decision rights / interfaces: May set first-line scientific standards and propose research resourcing within delegated budget. Cannot self-approve model validation or deployment, change risk limits, override compliance, or supersede the CDO's authority over data governance. Works with Quantitative Research Director, Chief Economist, CTO, CDO and Independent Model Risk Manager.
Integration / overlap: Distinct from the Chief Data Officer (data rights, sourcing, quality, governance), Quantitative Research Director (overall quant research programme) and Head of AI Systems (agent orchestration and generative-AI infrastructure).

51.2 Chief Economist
Staffing applicability: Conditional: global macro, multi-asset and interest-rate-sensitive mandates requiring a central economic research view.
Reports to: CIO; provides scenario analyses to CRO and Investment Committee; may direct the Macro Research Analyst and coordinate with the Macro Regime Forecaster.
Role purpose: Lead firm-wide macroeconomic analysis, establish consistent economic assumptions and communicate uncertainty across rates, inflation, growth, currencies and geopolitical scenarios.
Principal responsibilities:
- Own the economic research calendar, macro indicator definitions, forecast vintages and an explicit baseline/upside/downside scenario framework, preserving dissenting research views.
- Coordinate inflation, labour-market, productivity, fiscal-policy, monetary-policy, rates and currency analysis across geographic and investment teams.
- Develop coherent cross-asset transmission scenarios and distinguish observed releases, external consensus, analyst interpretation and genuinely out-of-sample forecasts.
- Maintain a dated macro assumption library covering publication lags, central-bank calendars, yield curves, currency regimes and data revisions.
- Coordinate geopolitical event research with the relevant specialist, distinguish factual developments from contested claims and avoid expressing party or electoral preferences on behalf of the firm.
- Brief the CIO, macro portfolio managers and independent risk teams on forecast uncertainty, structural breaks, tail scenarios and adverse outcomes.
- Evaluate economic forecast quality through documented hindsight reviews and comparison with simple or market-implied benchmarks.
Required deliverables: Monthly and event-driven global macro outlook; forecast vintage database; rates/inflation/FX scenario deck; cross-asset scenario transmission map; forecast-error review.
Performance indicators: Forecast calibration and revision timeliness; source accuracy; scenario coherence; documented uncertainty; decision-usefulness assessed independently of whether markets happened to move in the forecast direction.
Qualifications / expertise: Advanced macroeconomics, monetary and international economics, econometrics, cross-asset market experience and leadership of economic research.
Decision rights / interfaces: Owns the firm's coordinated research baseline, not portfolio trading decisions, regulatory policy or independent risk limits. A PM may document an alternative scenario within an approved investment mandate; the CRO independently determines risk treatment.
Integration / overlap: Provides leadership to existing Macro Research Analyst (6.3) and coordinates with Macro Portfolio Manager (4.7), Regime Forecasting Specialist (38.5) and Macro Regime Forecaster (50.9), rather than replacing their respective research/portfolio roles.

52.1 Research Portfolio Manager

Performance indicators: Milestone timeliness; percentage of research with documented hypotheses and termination criteria; reusable validated outputs; budget utilisation; avoidable duplicate effort and integrity of stopped-project records.

52.2 Alpha Factory Manager
Staffing applicability: Core when the firm runs a high-volume, multi-team systematic-alpha discovery process.
Reports to: Quantitative Research Director; partners with Research Portfolio Manager, Head of Research Engineering and independent Strategy Validation.
Role purpose: Run the operational pipeline that converts documented alpha hypotheses into reproducible research candidates for independent testing and, when warranted, proposed live promotion.
Principal responsibilities:
- Define standard intake stages for idea submission, data clearance, baselines, preliminary backtesting, peer review, reproducibility and independent validation referral.
- Assign research candidates to appropriate alpha, feature engineering, microstructure, alternative-data and signal-combination teams while recording intellectual provenance.
- Maintain immutable experiment identifiers, realistic cost assumptions and full testing histories, including unsuccessful trials, to support selection-bias controls.
- Monitor each candidate's status and evidence; identify near-duplicate signals, correlated exposures and opportunities to combine nonredundant discoveries.
- Coordinate engineering hand-off, shadow-book testing and readiness packages without treating preliminary high Sharpe or rapid backtest success as sufficient proof of deployable alpha.

Qualifications / expertise: Systematic investment research, quantitative testing, experiment management, market data and trading implementation constraints.
Decision rights / interfaces: Can approve progression between internal research stages and submit a candidate for independent validation; cannot approve production trading, waive model-risk findings, or increase capital limits. 'Alpha factory' is a proposed workflow title, not a verified job title or operating model of any named firm.
Integration / overlap: Research Portfolio Manager decides which projects receive resources; Alpha Factory Manager manages the technical research funnel and quality gates; Model Approval Board and authorised investment decision-makers control deployment.

Principal responsibilities:

- Collect PM capital requests and compare feasible allocations subject to independent limits, diversification, capacity and execution constraints.
- Document how assumptions, costs and scaling sensitivities affect proposed allocations; maintain decision histories and denied-capital rationales.
- Escalate material changes in model validity, strategy health or liquidity to the designated PM, CRO and investment committee.
Required deliverables: Strategy catalogue; standardised capital request pack; overlap and capacity report; allocation-options docket; approved-decision audit trail.
Performance indicators: Completeness and currency of strategy profiles; comparability of net performance metrics; evidence quality; allocation turnaround; unreported overlap and capacity exceptions.
Qualifications / expertise: Portfolio construction, systematic performance measurement, strategy capacity, cross-asset risk and research governance.
Decision rights / interfaces: Advisory and administrative; it does not itself allocate capital, approve model promotion or change strategy risk limits. Formal capital decisions remain with designated human authorities and appropriate independent risk review.
Integration / overlap: Provides the comparison interface; Strategy Allocation Manager proposes weights, Capital Allocation Manager proposes risk/capital budgets and Capital Allocation Committee Agent prepares committee materials.

52.4 Market Ecology Researcher
Staffing applicability: Conditional: strategies materially exposed to crowding, endogenous liquidity, participant behaviour or rapid competitive adaptation.
Reports to: Quantitative Research Director; works with Market Microstructure Researcher (5.12), Crowding Analyst (38.3) and trading teams.
Role purpose: Research how heterogeneous market participants, structural market design and interactions among strategies affect alpha persistence and liquidity.
Principal responsibilities:
- Study public or lawfully licensed proxies for participant classes, flows, order-book behaviour, dealer inventory and ETF/index rebalancing, stating limits on unobservable trader identities.
- Model feedback among liquidity provision, trend following, hedging and de-risking to identify hypotheses about transient dislocations, correlated exits and structural decay.
- Evaluate changes in tick rules, fee structures, execution technology and product adoption for effects on market ecology.
- Build falsifiable agent-based or reduced-form models and compare findings with empirically observed market outcomes, allowing for alternative explanations.
- Analyse prospective strategy interaction and likely crowding under scaling without assuming a fixed AUM threshold determines when the role becomes necessary.
Required deliverables: Market-ecology research reports; participant-proxy assumptions ledger; scenario and competition models; falsifiable alpha-decay hypotheses.
Performance indicators: Reproducibility; out-of-sample explanatory value; calibration to observed liquidity and price effects; usefulness of risk and capacity referrals.
Qualifications / expertise: Market microstructure, empirical asset pricing, statistical modelling, simulation and exchange-market structure.
Decision rights / interfaces: Research and scenario recommendations only; cannot claim access to confidential rival positions or direct trading decisions. CRO independently assesses any risk implications.
Integration / overlap: Distinct from the Crowding Analyst's direct measurement of crowding and the Market Microstructure Researcher's order-level studies; focuses on system-level participant interactions and strategic adaptation.

52.5 Transaction Network Researcher
Staffing applicability: Conditional: investment or risk research requiring multi-market flow and financial-network analysis.
Reports to: Quantitative Research Director; interfaces with Chief Economist, Data Organisation, Transaction Cost Analyst and Contagion Risk Analyst (41.4).
Role purpose: Develop lawful, empirically testable representations of how observable transaction flows and market connections transmit liquidity and price shocks.
Principal responsibilities:
- Construct time-aware graphs from approved public or licensed transaction, venue, asset, issuer and funding data; prevent reconstruction or disclosure of restricted participant information.
- Analyse network centrality, flow concentration, cross-market lead-lag, hedging chains and intermediation bottlenecks; qualify when nodes represent aggregate proxies rather than identifiable participants.
- Test whether network statistics improve liquidity, impact or cross-asset forecasts beyond conventional baselines after realistic latency and costs.
- Model disruption scenarios involving common venues, instruments, collateral pools and funding providers; distinguish causal claims from observed association.
- Maintain dataset entitlements, privacy controls, temporal provenance and model documentation for all network features.
Required deliverables: Versioned transaction/flow graph; source and permissions register; network-factor research; shock-propagation simulations and independent validation referrals.
Performance indicators: Network reconstruction integrity; legal data-use compliance; forecast calibration; incremental net information content; quality of uncertainty and sensitivity analysis.
Qualifications / expertise: Graph statistics, time-series econometrics, market microstructure, data engineering and financial-network modelling.
Decision rights / interfaces: Research only; no privileged access to confidential customer order flow without explicit legal authorisation and information-barrier review.
Integration / overlap: Researches the forecasting mechanism; Contagion Risk Analyst uses independently challenged dependency assumptions to assess portfolio stress.

52.6 Cross-Strategy Dependency Researcher
Staffing applicability: Core when multiple systematic strategy pods share instruments, features, funding or market-risk exposures.
Reports to: Quantitative Research Director; collaborates with Strategy Allocation Manager and independently reports material findings to CRO/Contagion Risk Analyst.
Role purpose: Identify why strategies with low average measured correlation may incur joint losses under shared market, data, execution or financing stresses.
Principal responsibilities:
- Map shared data vendors, features, model families, instruments, factor exposures, brokers, market liquidity and execution venues across strategy pods.
- Estimate rolling, conditional and tail dependencies using appropriate sample-size controls and robustness tests; distinguish unstable correlation from structural/common-cause dependence.
- Evaluate crowding, forced deleveraging, collateral spirals, outages and regime transitions as alternative explanations for correlated drawdowns.
- Produce cross-strategy stress scenarios with explicit uncertainty and counterfactual diversification proposals.
- Feed dependency findings into portfolio construction and independent risk reviews without altering investment risk limits or approving strategy allocations.
Required deliverables: Strategy dependency graph; joint-loss scenario report; shared-resource risk register; proposed diversification research; change alerts on emerging common exposures.
Performance indicators: Completeness of shared-dependency mapping; scenario reproducibility; joint-loss model calibration; early identification of hidden overlap; quality of CRO referrals.
Qualifications / expertise: Multivariate time series, portfolio risk, tail dependence, derivatives/financing mechanics and systematic strategy implementation.
Decision rights / interfaces: First-line research and advisory only; independent Correlation Monitoring Agent (10.10), Concentration Risk Specialist (41.1) and Contagion Risk Analyst (41.4) retain second-line review.
Integration / overlap: Focuses on causal research hypotheses across strategy designs; Portfolio Crowding Manager (39.4) acts on approved allocation proposals, and CRO assesses binding risk treatment.

53. NEW SPECIALISATIONS — TREASURY, REPO AND INTRADAY FUNDING
Reporting and oversight: Head of Financing / CFO for firm and approved fund funding; independent CRO monitors liquidity, collateral and counterparty limits; Operations independently reconciles movements.

53.1 Treasury Repo Specialist / Repo Financing Specialist
Staffing applicability: Conditional: approved government-bond, fixed-income, ETF, market-making or other portfolios use repurchase agreements or securities financing.
Reports to: Head of Financing / CFO; coordinates with Fixed Income Portfolio Manager, Market Making, Collateral Operations and Counterparty Risk.
Role purpose: Source, structure and monitor authorised repurchase agreement and reverse-repurchase financing with attention to eligible collateral, margin, counterparty exposure and rollover risk.
Principal responsibilities:
- Compare repo/reverse-repo quotes, maturities, secured funding spreads, eligible collateral, initial margins/haircuts and concentration across approved counterparties.
- Forecast repo funding needs for cash securities, ETF market-making inventory and collateral-intensive fixed-income exposures, including settlement and roll dates.
- Review collateral eligibility, substitution rights, margin calls, close-out provisions and legal agreement terms with treasury, operations and Legal.
- Assess term versus overnight funding trade-offs, refinancing cliffs, collateral scarcity, specials and liquidity under market stress.
- Prepare execution requests within approved funding mandates; reconcile confirmed transactions and collateral movements independently through Operations.
Required deliverables: Repo funding ladder; approved counterparty and collateral matrix; all-in secured financing comparison; rollover and haircut stress assessment; deal and maturity logs.
Performance indicators: All-in financing cost versus suitable benchmarks; successful funding rollovers; accurate maturity and margin forecasts; collateral eligibility compliance; zero unauthorised counterparties.
Qualifications / expertise: Repo market conventions, fixed-income instruments, collateral management, secured financing documentation and funding-liquidity risk.
Decision rights / interfaces: May negotiate/submit secured funding within delegated authority and approved counterparties; cannot pledge client assets beyond governing documents or bypass risk, legal, counterparty or collateral approvals.
Integration / overlap: Specialist execution/structure owner beneath Prime Broker Manager (46.1) and Financing Optimiser (46.2); Operations (30.2) independently processes collateral and CRO assesses liquidity risk.

53.2 Funding Liquidity Trader
Staffing applicability: Conditional: material intraday liquidity, cross-currency funding, leveraged market-making or financing books.
Reports to: Head of Financing / Treasurer under CFO; works with Trading for authorised instruments, Cash Management, Risk and Collateral Operations.
Role purpose: Implement the firm's approved day-to-day liquidity and funding plan in market conditions that may change materially intraday.
Principal responsibilities:
- Monitor actual cash, settlement flows, collateral calls, available credit, currency imbalances and intraday projected funding gaps by legal entity and fund.
- Compare approved sources of cash and secured funding by all-in cost, speed, maturity, haircut, availability and concentration; propose and execute authorised funding transactions.
- Maintain intraday funding ladders and contingency buffers; notify CRO and COO of liquidity-limit proximity, failed settlements, collateral shortfalls or counterparty disruptions.
- Coordinate FX funding, repo maturities, collateral substitutions and emergency funding requests with relevant specialists, subject to mandate and legal-entity restrictions.
- Log funding decisions and reconcile completed cash/securities movements with independent Operations and Finance controllers.
Required deliverables: Intraday funding dashboard; funding orders and confirmations; liquidity-source ladder; shortfall escalation record; end-of-day funding attribution.
Performance indicators: Funding availability; timely settlement support; all-in funding cost; realised intraday liquidity breaches; counterparty diversification and transaction reconciliation accuracy.
Qualifications / expertise: Short-term money markets, secured funding, intraday liquidity management, operational settlement windows and treasury controls.
Decision rights / interfaces: Executes only pre-authorised treasury transactions within prescribed instrument, notional, counterparty and legal-entity limits; cannot independently waive buffers, alter investment strategy, or instruct unauthorised fund transfers.
Integration / overlap: Trading and intraday funding IMPLEMENTATION role; Treasury Agent (15.5) handles wider treasury support and reporting, while Funding Liquidity Trader operates authorised short-term funding transactions. Repo Specialist provides instrument-specific expertise.

54. AI-NATIVE ORGANISATION AND INSTITUTIONAL KNOWLEDGE
Reporting and oversight: Head of AI Systems / CTO for workflow design; Chief of Staff and relevant division owners for business-process adoption; CDO/CISO/CCO govern records, rights and access.

54.1 Autonomous Workflow Designer
Staffing applicability: Core when the firm relies on multi-agent workflows for material research or operations.
Reports to: Head of AI Systems / CTO; partners with COO, business process owners, AI Systems Lead and independent control functions.
Role purpose: Redesign end-to-end work so appropriately scoped AI agents and human owners cooperate efficiently, with clear boundaries, evidence hand-offs and failure recovery.
Principal responsibilities:
- Map current workflows into inputs, decisions, hand-offs, tools, owners, approvals, exceptions and audit requirements; identify unnecessary duplication and fragile automated chains.
- Specify which subtasks require deterministic services, narrow-purpose agents, human review or prohibition of autonomous action; create task-decomposition and escalation designs.
- Define state machines, service contracts, structured outputs, transaction idempotency, timeout rules and controlled recovery after tool or model failure.
- Trial alternative workflow layouts in a sandbox; measure task correctness, latency, total cost, error propagation and unauthorised-action risk against a documented baseline.
- Coordinate operational change and training; ensure no workflow modification silently transfers authority from Risk, Compliance, independent validation or authorised human committees.
- Propose workflow updates through the existing Technology Governance and production change-control process, with independent testing of materially consequential actions.
Required deliverables: Current/future process maps; agent-human responsibility matrix; tool/approval boundary design; sandbox evaluation and failure-mode analysis; approved workflow-change plan.
Performance indicators: Validated end-to-end task quality; control compliance; cycle time; avoidable duplicate work; cost per completed verified task; incident and rollback rates.
Qualifications / expertise: Systems/process engineering, agent orchestration, software architecture, operational risk and human-in-the-loop control design.
Decision rights / interfaces: May design and recommend workflows; cannot expand agent permissions, waive approvals, deploy consequential changes or change trading risk limits without authorised review.
Integration / overlap: Designs organisational task architecture; Agent Orchestrator (13.1) executes approved workflows; Multi-Agent Coordinator (13.2) coordinates runtime collaboration; Prompt Engineer (13.7) develops task instructions.

54.2 Institutional Memory Curator
Staffing applicability: Core when the firm maintains multi-year research, model, investment and incident records across teams.
Reports to: Chief of Staff or Head of Research Operations for knowledge stewardship; accountable interfaces with CDO, General Counsel, CISO and Research Librarian.
Role purpose: Preserve verified institutional lessons and the rationale for past decisions so failed research, model limitations, investment history and incident remediation remain available to authorised future teams.
Principal responsibilities:
- Maintain a versioned, permissioned record of significant strategy approvals/rejections, research failures, model retirements, funding events, operational incidents and postmortems.
- Distinguish contemporaneous evidence, decision rationale, later reinterpretation and unresolved dissent; preserve the date, participants, approvals, provenance and known limitations of each lesson.
- Convert approved lessons into searchable retrieval summaries and cross-references to source documents, model versions and corrective-action records.
- Coordinate updates to workflow guidance and research checklists only after review by responsible process owners, avoiding unsupported inference from anecdotal past incidents.
- Apply retention, confidentiality, client-asset, MNPI, licensing and personal-data restrictions; enforce need-to-know retrieval and secure archival or deletion when required.
- Periodically check whether prior remediation remains effective and whether similar incidents or rejected hypotheses are recurring, without suppressing genuine independent re-testing.
Required deliverables: Institutional decision-and-lessons register; failed-research catalogue; incident-to-remediation knowledge map; permissions-aware retrieval collection; quarterly recurring-lessons briefing.
Performance indicators: Source traceability; completeness of material decision records; retrieval relevance for authorised users; recurrence visibility; access-policy compliance and error-correction timeliness.
Qualifications / expertise: Investment research process, records governance, knowledge management, provenance, secure retrieval and post-incident analysis.
Decision rights / interfaces: Curates authorised records; may flag contradictions or overdue lessons, but cannot edit original immutable evidence, change approved decisions or independently reclassify restricted information.
Integration / overlap: Research Librarian (5.19) catalogues research and publications; Knowledge Graph Manager (13.12) maintains entity relationships; Agent Memory Manager (13.6) manages technical context retention. Curator owns human-reviewed institutional lessons and decision context.

55. BOARD SECRETARIAT AND REGULATORY AFFAIRS
Reporting and oversight: Board/committee Chair and Company Secretary for governance administration; CCO/General Counsel for regulatory positions. Secretarial functions do not confer executive voting or regulatory sign-off authority.

55.1 Board Risk Secretary
Staffing applicability: Core when the firm has a formal board-level risk oversight committee; otherwise assign the function to the Company Secretary or qualified governance officer without compromising confidentiality or board access.
Reports to: Chair of the Board Risk Committee for committee matters; administratively to Company Secretary / Board Secretariat, not to a portfolio manager.
Role purpose: Maintain reliable governance records and committee workflows for board risk oversight, including documented challenge, decisions, follow-up and escalation.
Principal responsibilities:
- Agree the risk committee calendar and agenda with its Chair; obtain CRO materials and necessary independent reports within appropriate cut-offs.
- Validate that committee packs include relevant limit breaches, stress scenarios, liquidity and counterparty issues, material model findings and prior action status.
- Record attendees, quorum, conflicts, dissent, formal decisions, delegated authorities and deadlines; distinguish a committee resolution from an AI-generated summary.
- Maintain a controlled action register; request status evidence, escalate overdue critical actions and track closure as approved by the designated owner or committee.
- Manage board-only confidentiality, retention and access controls with Legal, the Company Secretary and CISO; coordinate cross-references to Audit and Governance committee decisions where authorised.
- Prepare accurate minutes for Chair review and formal approval; preserve supporting evidence and version history for independent audit and regulatory examinations where applicable.
Required deliverables: Risk Committee agenda and evidence packs; draft and approved minutes; conflicts/quorum record; action register; decision and escalation audit trail.
Performance indicators: Pack completeness and timeliness; minutes accuracy; action follow-up; missing-approval incidents; confidentiality and retention compliance.
Qualifications / expertise: Board governance administration, investment risk terminology, committee procedures, minute-taking and regulated records management.
Decision rights / interfaces: Administrative and evidence-control authority only; does not chair meetings, vote, change risk appetite, authorise waivers or substitute for CRO/board oversight.
Integration / overlap: Focused board-secretariat specialist; existing Risk Committee Agent (17.3) prepares analytical risk evidence and Board Audit Committee Liaison (32.1) supports audit governance.

55.2 Regulatory Affairs Director
Staffing applicability: Conditional: a multi-jurisdictional regulated firm with material policy-development engagement or regular consultation responses.
Reports to: CCO / General Counsel; coordinates with CEO, external government relations advisers and authorised industry associations.
Role purpose: Lead the firm's documented engagement with financial regulators and policy consultations on issues affecting its licensed entities, products, market access and investor obligations.
Principal responsibilities:
- Maintain a forward-looking regulatory-policy and consultation calendar across relevant jurisdictions, including FCA, SEC, ESMA and other authorities according to the firm's actual regulatory perimeter.
- Assess proposed rather than merely enacted rules; work with Legal, Risk, Technology and business heads to identify operational impacts, evidence gaps and implementation lead times.
- Prepare evidence-based consultation responses and technically accurate regulatory engagement briefs, clearly separating current law, supervisory guidance, proposed requirements and the firm's own policy preferences.
- Coordinate regulator meetings and industry-association participation with the CCO and Legal; maintain required records and manage lobbying registration, conflicts and disclosure requirements where applicable.
- Escalate major policy developments to executive management without allowing engagement strategy to delay compliance with existing obligations.
- Preserve a traceable record of formal submissions, regulator feedback and approved firm positions; communicate confirmed changes to Regulatory Monitoring and Compliance implementation owners.
Required deliverables: Regulatory-policy horizon scan; consultation response packages; regulator engagement register; cross-entity impact assessment; regulatory affairs briefing to the board.
Performance indicators: Timely and accurate consultation coverage; evidence quality; documented review and approval; regulatory engagement record completeness; on-time hand-off to compliance implementation.
Qualifications / expertise: Financial services regulatory policy, legal/regulatory analysis, consultations, market structure, stakeholder engagement and cross-border compliance.
Decision rights / interfaces: May coordinate drafting and engagement, but cannot make binding legal determinations, approve regulatory filings without delegated authority, change compliance obligations or represent personal/political preferences as the firm's adopted position.
Integration / overlap: Regulatory Monitoring Agent (14.2) detects and summarises regulatory developments; Regulatory Reporting Officer (29.3) owns applicable returns; Regulatory Affairs Director coordinates proposed-policy analysis, public consultation and external regulator engagement.
